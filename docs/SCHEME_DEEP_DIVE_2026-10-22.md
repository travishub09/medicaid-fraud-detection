# Scheme deep dive — why the schemes that do not separate the cases do not, what the model finds without a name, and what is not wired yet

_Written 2026-10-22 from the v19 packet (SCHEME_HEALTH, SCHEME_TRIAGE, CASE_VALIDATION, LABEL_ENRICHMENT, LEARNING_LOOP near misses, SOURCE_ABLATION, NETWORK_AB) and the scheme table in code. Every number is a measurement on the 2021-12 freeze graded against the FCA ruler (DOJ / OIG outcomes announced in the 36 months after the freeze, 1,008 forward positives in a 614,328-provider universe). Leads for counsel review, never accusations; a scheme verdict here is a statement about a calculation, not about any provider._

## 1. The question, and how it is answered

The operator's question has three parts: for each scheme that does not separate the cases, is the calculation wrong, is the data missing, or is the scheme simply not an indicator of the fraud that ends in a False Claims Act outcome; what is the model using that no scheme names; and what does the industry know that is not wired in.

The platform already measures most of this on every run, and the honest finding is that nobody had read the measurements together. Four instruments exist:

| instrument | what it answers | file |
|---|---|---|
| scheme health | does the subscore separate the exclusion label and the forward FCA ruler (AUC), on how many positives, with which inputs present | SCHEME_HEALTH.md / scheme_health.json |
| scheme triage | the six questions per underperformer: symptom, design, missing fields, calc findings, wiring, files | SCHEME_TRIAGE.md |
| case validation | does the subscore rank the PROVEN DOJ cases of its own conduct type above everyone else (the ruler that fits a billing scheme) | CASE_VALIDATION.md |
| label enrichment | where the forward cases concentrate: taxonomy, size, codes, each scheme's top decile | LABEL_ENRICHMENT.md |

Read together they give a diagnosis class per scheme:

- **A. calculation or design defect** — the inputs exist but the subscore cannot discriminate because of how it is built (near-constant inputs, a percentile of a binary flag, a single input).
- **B. inputs missing** — the scheme runs on a fraction of its intended inputs or on none (a file not procured, a builder never written).
- **C. wrong population** — the scheme is computed on a universe the FCA ruler barely touches (individual prescribers on Medicare files) while the forward cases are organizations (82% entity type 2, lift 4 for organizations with over 50 providers).
- **D. not an indicator of the FCA-case shape as built** — inputs present, calculation sound, population right, and still no separation.
- **E. unjudged** — too few forward positives in coverage to say anything (thin).
- **F. signal** — separates the forward cases.

**Reading the column of lifts.** A scheme can have a flat AUC and an enriched top decile at the same time (behavioral_health, impossible_day, contract_pharmacy). AUC averages over the whole ranking, including the half of the universe the scheme leaves unscored; the top decile is what a lead list experiences. The learner routes around flat subscores, which is why the prune list is graded as an A/B before anything is dropped.

## 3. The cross-cutting findings

**Finding 1: half the "coin-flip" schemes are aimed at the wrong population.** pharma_kickback, pill_mill, upcoding, drug_outlier, overutilization and payment_outlier draw their coverage from the Medicare physician files (Part B, Part D, the opioid prescriber file, Open Payments): individual NPIs. The forward FCA ruler is 82% organizations and the owner-run operator book is organizations by construction. A scheme can be a true indicator of individual-prescriber fraud and still read as a coin flip on a ruler of settled organizations. The fix is measurement first: grade each scheme's FCA AUC separately for individuals and organizations (added to scheme_health today as `fca_auc_ind` / `fca_auc_org`), and only then decide whether the scheme needs an organization-side input or a prune.

**Finding 2: near-constant inputs make a percentile meaningless.** within_2_hops_of_exclusion (97% one value), related_party_density (96%), contract_pharmacy_concentration (97%), px_opioid_la_share (99%), growth_level_shift (98% ties in its top decile), rx_spread_top_share (81%), code_concentration_hhi (72%). A one-sided percentile of a column that is zero for nearly everyone is a tie block with a thin tail; the subscore that blends it inherits the ties. These inputs are flags, not measures. The right treatment is the one already used for the deactivation flags (RAW_SUBSCORE_INPUTS: the flag drives the subscore raw) or a count that has a distribution (number of excluded parties within two hops, not a 0/1).

**Finding 3: the inputs that are missing are the ones the typology needs.** Three scheme inputs have no producer (bene_per_day_p95, dme_ordering_md_concentration, local_volume_implausibility: RESERVED since the audit); dme_ring runs on one input of three because the referral slice and the pre-cutoff DocGraph do not exist; drug_outlier on four of six; hospice on five of seven; invalid_identity without the Death Master File; ownership_turnover without two owner snapshots (now archived monthly). None of these is a mystery: each has a named file or builder in docs/platform/12.

**Finding 4: the schemes that validate are sector schemes with sector data.** worthless_services (PBJ staffing, deficiencies, fines) validates at 0.94 on its proven cases; hospice_ineligibility separates the forward ruler at 0.84. Both read a public quality file that describes the facility, not a billing percentile. The lesson generalizes: the next schemes that will validate are the ones with a sector file behind them (home health Care Compare, hospice utilization, dialysis facility compare, DME supplier directory), not another ratio of the spending fact.

**Finding 5: the exclusion label and the FCA ruler disagree on what fraud looks like, and the schemes were tuned to the first.** The exclusion label is individuals excluded for program-related crimes; the FCA ruler is organizations that settled. single_service_mill is the sharpest example (inverted on both because the ruler's population is diversified). This is why the class priors are the single most important v21 change: they give the scorer the population base rate the percentiles strip out.

Schemes the industry names that the data in hand cannot see, so they are NOT candidates: same-day duplicate claims and modifier abuse (the fact is monthly and carries no modifiers), telehealth place-of-service misuse (no place of service), risk-adjustment upcoding (managed-care encounter data), unbundled surgical globals (claims-level), and anything inside T-MSIS research files (barred by the data-use rules in docs/platform/02).

**Data worth procuring for the sector-file pattern that validates.** Home Health Care Compare (quality measures and star ratings), the Hospice utilization and quality files already partly in hand, Dialysis Facility Compare, the DME supplier directory, the state MFCU annual reports as a label source (not a feature), and the pre-cutoff DocGraph editions (2015 to 2021) for referral edges under a freeze.

## 6. What to do, in order

1. **Measure before pruning.** scheme_health now reports the FCA AUC split by entity type; v21's packet will say which coin-flip schemes are coin-flip because of population (class C) and which are flat on both populations (class D). The ab_prune arm grades the scorer without the candidates.
2. **Fix the class-A inputs** (one bundle): treat the near-constant flags as raw subscore inputs or replace them with counts (excluded parties within two hops, related parties, 340B arrangements), and give behavioral_health the capacity input it is missing (patients per rendering clinician from the capacity check).
3. **Wire the computable schemes in two batches**, each a new pre-registration graded on its own ruler: batch one = personal_care_hcbs, home_health_intensity, lab_unbundling, psychotherapy_duration (the sectors with the most unresolved settlement dollars and enriched codes); batch two = aba_hours, dental_mill (after the D-code check), wound_care, dme_diabetic_supplies, new_patient_churn, ambulance_dialysis.
4. **Decode the billing embeddings** (a tool, no run): the top-loading HCPCS codes per dimension, so the latent factors the model leans on become named candidates.
5. **Procure the sector files** (home health, dialysis, the DME directory, pre-cutoff DocGraph) and the two label rulers the Medicaid-specific schemes need (state MFCU outcomes, the state termination lists already loading).
6. **Fill the class-B inputs** where the file exists: the Death Master File for billing_after_death, the NDC claim slice for the drug spread, the second owner snapshot for ownership_turnover (archiving monthly since 2026-10-21).

_Nothing in this document changes a label, a weight or a run. Every change above lands through a pre-registered run graded on the FCA ruler, and every scheme stays explainable by its named inputs._
